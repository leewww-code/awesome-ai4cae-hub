SHIFT-Truck: A High-Fidelity Aerodynamics Dataset and Benchmark for Pickup Trucks

arXiv 2026 47.6 method

TLDR

SHIFT-Truck introduces a 1,000-case high-fidelity SA-DDES pickup-truck aerodynamics dataset and neural-surrogate benchmark, validated against wind-tunnel data.

Reasoning

The paper's main strength is a large, high-fidelity, domain-specific CFD dataset with multiple neural-surrogate benchmarks and wind-tunnel validation. Its apparent limitations are that it uses one reference pickup geometry morphed across shape parameters and the abstract does not summarize surrogate performance or dataset diversity. Overall, it is highly relevant to CFD surrogates and automotive aerodynamics, but not to airfoil, wing-design, or LLM-agent topics.

Deep Analysis

Innovations

  • Introduces SHIFT-Truck, described as the first high-fidelity aerodynamics dataset and benchmark for pickup trucks, capturing open cargo bed flow absent from DrivAerML and SHIFT-SUV.
  • Provides 1,000 Spalart-Allmaras delayed detached-eddy simulations of a reference pickup geometry morphed across 17 shape parameters, at Reynolds number 1.4e7 on meshes of about 100 million cells.
  • Releases time-averaged surface pressure, wall shear stress, volumetric pressure, and velocity for each simulation.
  • Defines geometry-grouped splits and benchmarks four neural surrogates, DoMINO, GeoTransolver, AB-UPT, and SMART, on surface and volume tracks.
  • Introduces controlled distribution shifts in operating point, input surface discretization, and vehicle archetype to evaluate generalization beyond in-distribution accuracy.

Methodology

SHIFT-Truck consists of 1,000 SA-DDES simulations of a reference pickup geometry morphed across 17 shape parameters, each using about 100 million cells at a Reynolds number of 1.4e7. The setup is verified by grid refinement, repeated runs, and wind-tunnel checks, and the dataset includes time-averaged surface and volumetric flow fields. Four neural surrogates are benchmarked using geometry-grouped splits on surface and volume prediction tracks, with additional controlled distribution shifts in operating point, surface discretization, and vehicle archetype.

Key Results

Models with strong in-distribution performance can degrade substantially under the introduced distribution shifts: operating-condition changes expose failures to infer speed dependence, while tessellation and cross-vehicle shifts reveal markedly different robustness across architectures.

Limitations

  • Strong in-distribution surrogate performance does not guarantee generalization; models can degrade substantially under operating-point, tessellation, and cross-vehicle distribution shifts.
  • Operating-condition changes expose failures to infer speed dependence.
  • Robustness under tessellation and cross-vehicle shifts varies markedly across architectures, indicating no uniform robustness among the tested models.
