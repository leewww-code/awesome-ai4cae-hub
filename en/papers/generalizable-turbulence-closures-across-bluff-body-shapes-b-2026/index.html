Generalizable turbulence closures across bluff-body shapes by PINN-based solver-agnostic training

arXiv 2026 40.2 method

TLDR

The paper trains neural turbulence closures inside a solver-agnostic PINN using RANS residuals, achieving generalizable improvements over SST k-omega for 2D bluff-body wakes.

Reasoning

The paper's core strength is a solver-agnostic PINN training scheme for data-driven RANS closures, with rapid hypothesis screening and LOSO generalization across six bluff-body wakes. Weaknesses are limited scope in the abstract: only 2D bluff-body wakes at Re=10^4, no airfoil/wing/design or agentic/LLM methods, and claims are simulation-based.

Deep Analysis

Innovations

  • PINN-based inverse training of turbulence closures that imposes the RANS residual by automatic differentiation, making calibration mesh-free, differentiable, and solver-agnostic.
  • Removes the CFD solver/adjoint/ensemble loop during training, requiring only the final closure to be solver-stable and enabling arbitrary neural closures without adjoint computations.
  • Develops four closure forms: a local tensor-basis Reynolds stress model, a non-local model transporting TKE and recovering the out-of-plane normal stress, a learned-length-scale variant, and a Reynolds force model free of the realizability constraint.
  • Introduces input-gradient smoothing and a Lipschitz constraint to stabilize frozen closure deployment in a standard finite-element solver.
  • Demonstrates leave-one-shape-out generalization across six bluff-body wakes and efficient training on PIV data for geometries intractable for DNS.

Methodology

Closures are trained inside a physics-informed neural network by imposing Reynolds-averaged Navier-Stokes residuals via automatic differentiation, avoiding forward CFD solves during training. Four closure models are trained across six two-dimensional bluff-body wakes at Re = 10^4 and deployed frozen in a finite-element solver with input-gradient smoothing and a Lipschitz constraint. Evaluation uses a strict leave-one-shape-out protocol against a steady SST k-omega baseline, with accuracy assessed on stress fields, mean velocity, and drag.

Key Results

Under leave-one-shape-out, all four closures improve substantially over the steady SST k-omega baseline. The learned-length-scale closure is most accurate on stress fields, while the Reynolds force model generalizes best on mean velocity and drag, achieving about 8.5% LOSO drag error.

Limitations

  • Evaluation is limited to six two-dimensional bluff-body wakes at Re = 10^4.
  • Solver stability after frozen deployment is addressed by input-gradient smoothing and a Lipschitz constraint; no broader solver-stability guarantee is stated.
