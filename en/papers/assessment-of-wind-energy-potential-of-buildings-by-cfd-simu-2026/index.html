Deep Analysis

Innovations

  • Measurement-validated CFD framework for identifying optimal micro-wind turbine locations on buildings in dense urban environments
  • Coupling of multi-month on-site anemometry with a calibrated steady RANS digital twin using the realisable k-ε turbulence model
  • Architecturally integrated accelerator wing (aerofoil) placed at the envelope edge to amplify and stabilise local wind flow
  • Decision dendrogram design framework mapping wind-rose typology, building plan, height, and implantation to recommended device families
  • Capacity-factor analysis using measured Weibull distribution and Savonius rotor power curve to produce realistic annual yield estimates

Methodology

The methodology couples a multi-month on-site anemometry campaign on two Parisian Haussmannian buildings with a calibrated steady RANS CFD digital twin using the realisable k-ε turbulence model, and introduces an aerofoil at the envelope edge. Validation against year-long anemometer records uses MAPE and FAC2 against COST 732 acceptance criteria. A capacity-factor analysis uses the measured Weibull distribution and a Savonius rotor power curve, and findings are synthesized into a decision dendrogram.

Key Results

The CFD model achieves a mean absolute percentage error of approximately 18% and a hit-rate FAC2 above 0.85, meeting COST 732 acceptance criteria. The inter-tower case shows a directional speed-up factor of 3.4 (95% CI 3.18-3.62) at +70 m for the prevailing north-westerly sector; adding the aerofoil yields a further +13% local speed-up, equivalent to +44% extractable power, and the annual yield estimate is approximately 69 MWh, offsetting around 6% of building electricity consumption.

Limitations

  • Validation is reported at the level of MAPE ≈18% and FAC2 >0.85; other validation metrics or spatial error details are not provided in the abstract.
  • The findings are specific to dense Parisian Haussmannian blocks and the ENSAPVS inter-tower case study.
  • The capacity-factor and annual yield estimates are based on a Savonius rotor power curve and measured Weibull distribution, making them device- and site-specific.
  • The dendrogram is presented as a design-stage tool, but its performance across other urban morphologies is not reported in the abstract.
