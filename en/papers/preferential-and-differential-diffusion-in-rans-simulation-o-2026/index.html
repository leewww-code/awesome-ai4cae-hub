Deep Analysis

Innovations

  • Extends a tabulated-chemistry model that includes preferential and differential diffusion effects to a Reynolds-averaged Navier-Stokes (RANS) framework.
  • Derives transport equations for progress variable and mixture fraction from species mass transport equations considering mixture-averaged diffusion and Soret effect.
  • Incorporates turbulence-chemistry interaction via a presumed probability density function approach.
  • Assesses the impact of preferential and differential diffusion at thermochemical and transport levels by comparing with a unity-Lewis-number variant and a model with thermo-diffusive effects only in the flamelet table, and evaluates turbulence closures for turbulent diffusion, scalar dissipation rate, and Reynolds stresses.

Methodology

The study extends a tabulated-chemistry model to RANS using transport equations for progress variable and mixture fraction derived from species mass transport with mixture-averaged diffusion and Soret effect, and a presumed PDF for turbulence-chemistry interaction. It evaluates the model on a lean premixed H2-air slot burner at Reynolds numbers 5500 and 11000, using direct numerical simulation as reference. Comparisons include a unity-Lewis-number variant and a model with thermo-diffusive effects only in the flamelet table, plus assessment of turbulence closures.

Key Results

RANS simulations including preferential-differential diffusion correctly reproduce the DNS flame length, heat-release distribution, and characteristic equivalence-ratio and super-adiabatic temperature branches of the slot flame. The comparisons demonstrate the impact of preferential and differential diffusion at thermochemical and transport levels and the model's capability in cost-effective RANS simulations of lean hydrogen flames.
