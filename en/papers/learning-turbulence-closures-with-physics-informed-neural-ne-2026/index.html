Learning Turbulence Closures with Physics-Informed Neural Networks for the Rayleigh-Taylor Transition to Turbulence

arXiv 2026 38.1 method

TLDR

A physics-informed neural network calibrates and corrects a k-ε-b RANS turbulence closure, improving Rayleigh-Taylor transition predictions against DNS data.

Reasoning

The paper targets a clear gap in RANS modeling of Rayleigh-Taylor transition and proposes an interpretable PINN-based correction framework, evaluated on DNS data. Strengths include embedding the closure in a PINN and re-expressing learned corrections as analytical modifications. Limitations include validation only against DNS rather than physical experiments and unclear generalization beyond the considered k-ε-b model and flow regime from the abstract.

Deep Analysis

Innovations

  • Embedding a simple k-epsilon-b turbulence model within a surrogate physics-informed neural network to enable data-driven closure enhancement.
  • Using the PINN-based framework to calibrate coefficients for parametric errors and identify corrective terms for structural errors from missing physical processes.
  • Re-expressing learned corrections as explicit analytical functions of model state variables and flow indicators, yielding a fully interpretable corrected closure.
  • Demonstrating improved Rayleigh-Taylor mixing-layer growth predictions during the transition to turbulence against direct numerical simulation data.

Methodology

A simple k-epsilon-b turbulence model is embedded in a surrogate physics-informed neural network. The framework calibrates model coefficients and identifies structural corrective terms, which are then re-expressed as explicit analytical modifications depending on model state variables and flow indicators. The corrected model is evaluated against an extensive database of direct numerical simulations of Rayleigh-Taylor flows.

Key Results

The resulting fully interpretable corrected turbulence model enables improved predictions of Rayleigh-Taylor mixing-layer growth during the transition to turbulence when assessed against direct numerical simulation data.
