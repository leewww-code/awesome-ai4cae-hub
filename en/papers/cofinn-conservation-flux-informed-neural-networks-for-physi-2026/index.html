Reasoning

Deep Analysis

Innovations

  • Introduces CoFINN, a physics-informed deep learning framework that embeds finite-volume conservation physics directly into CNN training.
  • Interprets CNN output fields as structured computational grids where each pixel is a finite-volume cell and enforces conservation consistency through numerical flux calculations.
  • Uses a conservation-based loss as a physical regularizer, particularly beneficial in limited-data regimes.
  • Differs from conventional CNNs that optimize only pixel-wise similarity metrics and from classical physics-informed methods that enforce differential-equation residuals via automatic differentiation.
  • Architecture-agnostic and extensible to broader classes of conservation-law-governed physical systems.

Methodology

CoFINN trains a CNN surrogate by treating its output fields as finite-volume computational grids and enforcing conservation consistency through numerical flux calculations embedded in the loss. The framework is evaluated on transonic flow prediction around airfoils at M=0.7 and Re=6*10^6, including shock waves and high angles of attack. It is compared against conventional data-driven CNNs using aerodynamic force prediction accuracy metrics such as drag prediction error.

Key Results

CoFINN improved aerodynamic force prediction accuracy, reducing drag prediction error by up to 34% at extreme angles of attack and by approximately 15% on average across the test set, with especially significant gains in limited-data regimes.
