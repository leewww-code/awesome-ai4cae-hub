Do Better Scores Mean Better Physics? Physics-Grounded Explanations for Sim2Real Neural Operators

arXiv 2026 47.1 method

TLDR

Study finds neural-operator benchmark errors can misalign with physically relevant flow-statistics errors for NACA4418 airfoil CFD/PIV data, motivating complementary physical diagnostics.

Reasoning

The paper uses paired CFD simulations and experimental PIV data for a NACA4418 airfoil, testing four neural operators with interventions and stress tests to probe physical relevance beyond aggregate error. Its main limitation is that it studies one benchmark and explicitly notes the intervention measures sensitivity, not counterfactual physical correctness. It does not address airfoil design, optimization, turbulence modeling, or agentic/LLM workflows.

Deep Analysis

Innovations

  • Physics-grounded input interventions that remove velocity fluctuations from selected regions of observed flow histories to probe neural operator sensitivity
  • Mean-preserving masking procedure comparing removal from the most energetic 10% of valid observed cells to equal-area random removal
  • Output attenuation stress test for assessing disagreement between benchmark error and domain-summed fluctuation energy
  • Advocacy for reporting complementary physical diagnostics alongside aggregate prediction scores for sim2real neural operators

Methodology

The study uses paired computational-fluid-dynamics simulations and experimental particle-image-velocimetry measurements for flow around a NACA4418 airfoil. Four neural operators are evaluated using mean-preserving input interventions that remove velocity fluctuations from the most energetic 10% of valid observed cells versus equal-area random removal. Reported metrics include velocity-field error and two-component fluctuation-energy error, with a CNO compared against a reference and an output attenuation stress test.

Key Results

Removing fluctuations from the most energetic 10% of valid observed cells changes forecasts more than equal-area random removal, though this is interpreted as sensitivity because masks are not matched for removed fluctuation energy. A CNO has lower velocity-field error but substantially higher two-component fluctuation-energy error than the reference on both analysis subsets, and the output attenuation stress test shows disagreement between benchmark errors and domain-summed fluctuation energy.

Limitations

  • The intervention masks are not matched for removed fluctuation energy, so the contrast measures sensitivity rather than independent evidence of physical importance.
  • Results are based on a single benchmark and do not establish counterfactual physical correctness.
  • The findings motivate reporting complementary physical diagnostics but do not establish a causal link between better benchmark scores and better physics.
